The Digit Count Rule
The product of an m-digit number and an n-digit number always has exactly (m+n-1) or (m+n) digits.
m-digit × n-digit = (m+n−1) or (m+n) digits

Try This
Pattern: Tap the "111×111" preset. Product = 12,321 — a palindrome! Now try 1111×1111 = 1,234,321. See the pattern?
Digit Rule: Set A=99, B=99. Product = 9,801 (4 digits). Set A=10, B=10. Product = 100 (3 digits). Both are 2-digit × 2-digit, but products have 3 OR 4 digits. The rule says: 2+2-1=3 or 2+2=4. Verified!
Shortcuts: To multiply 116 × 5, don't do it directly. Instead: 116 ÷ 2 = 58, then 58 × 10 = 580. Much faster!
Digit Grid: Try A=100 (3-digit), B=100 (3-digit). Product = 10,000 (5 digits). The grid says 3+3-1=5 or 3+3=6. It's 5 digits — the minimum! Why? Because 100 is the smallest 3-digit number.
Key Concept
When you multiply an m-digit number by an n-digit number, the product always has either (m+n-1) or (m+n) digits. To find the minimum product, multiply the smallest numbers (10×10=100, 3 digits). To find the maximum, multiply the largest (99×99=9801, 4 digits).
